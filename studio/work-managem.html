Redesigning ManaGEM, the business management system a dozen companies run on

ManaGEM began as the system GEMIS built for SBS Waterproofing and now runs the day-to-day operations of about a dozen companies. In 2026 the studio redesigned its interface end to end — every module, from the first enquiry to the last invoice — as a working, clickable prototype.

02 · The brief

One system for
the whole business.

As other companies came onto the platform, it had to serve many businesses without each one bending to it — and keep every enquiry, quote, invoice, project, stock level and staff record in one place.

The redesign had to make that breadth feel simple: one way in, one way of finding things, and the same patterns on every screen.

03 · The challenge

Where the work
was leaking.

ManaGEM began as the system GEMIS built for SBS Waterproofing, because the off-the-shelf apps the business tried did not fit how a contractor sells and delivers work: an enquiry becomes a site visit, a quote, a deposit, a scheduled job and a final invoice, with stock consumed along the way. Each step lived in a different tool, so leads were lost between them and nobody could see which jobs made money.

What the origin system had to answer:

  • Enquiries were arriving faster than anyone could keep track of them.
  • After a quote was accepted, the deposit and the accounting entry depended on someone remembering to raise them.
  • Nobody could see which jobs made money.
  • A generic package would have forced the business to work the package’s way.
04 · What we designed

The work, in the order
it happens.

Each module follows the sequence a sale actually takes — enquiry, quote, deposit, job, invoice — so the screens read in the same order as the work.

01 / 07

Every sale starts as one enquiry

Make the enquiry the root record, captured once, with SMS and email updates to the customer. A single record that every call, email and appointment hangs off means nothing is lost between tools, and the customer has confirmation without a phone call.

The New Enquiry screen captures it in four steps — customer, site and scope, schedule, review — so nothing is left for later.

02 / 07

A pipeline you can read at a glance

Open enquiries are grouped by where they stand — new lead, quoted, deposit paid, in progress, invoiced — with the count on each, and filters by sales rep, area and type. Closed enquiries keep the outcome and the win rate.

03 / 07

Quotes in a few clicks, deposits that follow

Quotes go out in a few clicks and are accepted online; the deposit invoice follows automatically. The hand-offs that leak time are the ones that rely on memory; automating them removes re-keying and the delay before a job can start.

Quote templates carry the terms and headings; invoices show what was billed, what is paid and what is overdue.

04 / 07

Accounting that stays in step

In the record, the deposit invoice follows an accepted quote straight into accounting. The redesign gives that link its own screen: what has synced, what is waiting, what failed, and the VAT for the period.

05 / 07

The profit on every job

Hold the costs with the sale and show the profit on every project. Management decisions should come from live job data rather than a reconstruction at year-end.

Current jobs show their progress, foreman and dates; the dashboard keeps live projects, recent sales and the week’s bookings in view.

06 / 07

Stock and the factory floor

Stock control brings stock value, low-stock alerts, movements and open purchase orders together; the factory follows each build through a production pipeline to invoicing.

07 / 07

Who owes what, and what the numbers say

Debtors management shows outstanding balances, ageing and statements; reports cover enquiries, quotes, opportunities, sales and product sales from the same live data.

05 · The interface

One set of
components.

Every module is built from the same pieces: summary cards with their trend, pipeline stages with their counts, status chips beside every amount, and a toolbar that filters by period and exports to CSV, Excel and PDF.

A user who has learnt one screen has learnt them all — which is what lets one system carry this many modules and still serve businesses other than the one it was built for.

06 · Build and handoff

Designed and built
under one roof.

Built and run by GEMIS’s own developers: the same team that designs it ships it, which is how a system built for one business in 2015 became a product about a dozen companies run on. The 2026 redesign is the studio’s Interface Refresh Sprint applied to the product GEMIS already builds; its component spec and handoff are internal to the one roof.
