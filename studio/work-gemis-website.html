Rebuilding the GEMIS website so every claim shows its proof

GEM Information Systems has built business systems in South Africa since 2014 — more than fifty of them, across five or more sectors. In 2026 the studio rebuilt the company’s own website to show that work rather than describe it: every page leads with a real project, and every claim traces to a record.

GEM Information Systems — the studio’s own company

Every screen on this page is the GEMIS website as published. The projects it shows are GEMIS’s real case studies; the interfaces inside them are stills from each project’s case-study video, illustrated for the video.

02 · The brief

A company that builds systems
should show them.

GEMIS designs, builds and supports the business systems South African companies run on — enquiries, jobs, claims, stock, reporting — on the Microsoft Cloud, and stays with them for years. The website had to say that in its first lines, and then prove it.

The brief: lead with the work, name the service, the market and the proof, and give every visitor one clear next step.

03 · The challenge

A site that described
instead of showing.

The site had grown page by page, and the audit that opened the redesign read every page and every asset before anything changed. What it found:

  • The home page said what GEMIS believes, not what it builds, for whom, or the proof.
  • Its client section was made of placeholder quotations, and no project was featured.
  • Case study cards named the industry, but not GEMIS’s role, the project’s status or what was built.
  • The one action had four names, none of them in the bar, and body copy was set at 14px.
  • The stylesheet had been written page by page, and its layers contradicted each other.
04 · What we designed

Proof first,
then the pitch.

Every page was rebuilt around the evidence GEMIS already had — seven case-study videos, the project records and a short list of confirmed figures — and nothing was written that those records do not support.

01 / 07

A home page that leads with proof

The hero names the service, the market and the proof; the three confirmed figures sit under the actions, and the client marks follow straight after. Then the nine disciplines, how GEMIS works, and seven systems in production, each with its story on video.

The order alternates navy and white, so no two dark sections meet.

02 / 07

One case card, on every page

Every project is shown by the same card: a numeral and the industry, a still from the project’s video with its duration and a play control, the name, one line on what it does, the status and one action.

The Case Studies index, the home page, the related rows, the service pages and the articles all use it — and the index filters it by industry and by what the system had to do.

03 / 07

Case studies that show their working

Each case study runs the same way: a project snapshot, the business challenge, what the design was based on, the workflow as a diagram, the system step by step in stills from its video, the key decisions with their reasons, and the outcomes split into delivered, verified and intended.

Where the record is silent — no research report, no measured result — the page says so rather than guessing.

04 / 07

Nine disciplines, each with its evidence

Solutions opens on one drawing — nine disciplines into one accountable team — then a numbered index of what each is for and when to choose it.

Each of the nine service pages has its own drawing and its own order of sections, and its evidence names only the projects whose record shows that work, with a line on why each is relevant there.

05 / 07

Evidence on the page that makes the offer

A visitor reading about system integration sees the integration GEMIS has run — the Trans Hex financial import, scheduled, de-duplicated and logged — with GEMIS’s role, the status and a link to the full story, without leaving the page.

06 / 07

One action, named the same everywhere

Four names for the same step became one: Book a discovery session — the last item in the bar on every page, and the end of every route.

The form checks each field as the visitor leaves it, lists every problem in one summary that takes focus, asks for consent in plain words, and says what will happen when it is sent.

07 / 07

The same site on a phone

Every page was checked from 360px to 1920px wide with no sideways scroll. On a phone the sub-navigation keeps only the page’s name and the action, and the case cards stack in one column.

05 · The interface

One design
language.

Midnight navy and white, one typeface, uppercase light headings, small tracked labels, square buttons, and blocks cut with a stepped notch. Where a stock photograph would have gone there is a pictogram — a silhouette filled with fine vertical lines — so every image on the site is either a drawing or the real work.

Every button on a navy ground is white. Every number read as a number is a light numeral. The decisions live in one place at the end of one stylesheet, so a change reaches every page.

06 · Build and handoff

Built so the facts
live in one place.

The site is static HTML generated from data files: the project records, the solutions, the confirmed figures and one SEO record. A fact is changed once, and every page that carries it follows.

Each page has a unique title and description and one structured-data graph, written from that SEO record along with the sitemap. Analytics load only after a visitor accepts. There is no phone number anywhere on the site. It is served by GitHub Pages.

07 · Results

Checked on
every change.

30

pages, each with its own title, description and structured data

0

serious or critical accessibility issues, on any page (axe)

0

broken internal links across the site

These are the checks the site is held to before any change ships: accessibility, no sideways scroll from 360px to 1920px wide, the internal links, the shared header and footer, and the SEO record.

These are build checks, not business results. Visits, enquiries and search rankings since the rebuild have not been measured yet, so none are claimed.
